Initial Central Question: Are there patterns in ED visits that appear in patients that are later diagnosed with bladder cancer? 

1) Quick round (5 min)  ##############
- Each person: one sentence on what the stakeholder needs from this dataset.

2) Stakeholder persona (10 min)  ##############
- Who are they (role and context)? --> Our stakeholder is a person who oversees the Genitourinary Oncology Department. 

- What do they care about (top 3 priorities)?  
1. How often are patients misdiagnosed with UTIs or other urological conditions before they are identified correctly with Bladder Cancer?
2. How often is Bladder Cancer detected in the later stages?
3. Are there any underlying patterns that can be avoided in diagnosing Bladder Cancer? 

- What constraints do they have (time, budget, risk tolerance)?

Supporting Source: https://worldbladdercancer.org/news_events/press-release-more-than-half-of-people-with-bladder-cancer-are-misdiagnosed-with-another-disease/

1. Time: It would take a lot of time to retrain specialists to avoid patterns of misdiagnosing Bladder Cancer. 
2. Risk Tolerance: If our patterns contribute to misdiagnoses/underdiagnoses, it could lead to multiple lawsuits. 
3. Budget: Retraining specialists require money to be spent, given that staff are already overworked.

Stakeholder persona = Head of the Genitourinary Oncology Department
